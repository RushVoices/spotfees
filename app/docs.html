01

Overview

On pump.fun, every trade pays the coin’s creator, and only the creator. That one seat collects fees for as long as the coin lives. Spot opens it up: creators sell a share of their future fees for SOL on day one, and the people who believe in a coin get a spot in what it earns.

Every pump.fun coin pays its creator a fee on every trade. Spot splits that fee stream into ten equal shares, called Stacks, and sells them in an auction. Whoever holds a Stack receives 10% of the coin’s creator fees, in SOL, for as long as the coin trades.

The split is set once, at launch, inside Pump’s own fee-sharing program, and then locked. Nothing about owning, buying or claiming a Stack touches Pump again. The Spot program only keeps track of who owns each of the ten vaults and lets that owner withdraw.

02

What a Stack is

A Stack is a record in the Spot program: launch.owners[i], for i from 0 to 9. Each index has its own vault, an on-chain address that only the program can pay out of. Pump pays 10% of the coin’s creator fees into each vault.

Per coin10 Stacks
Share each10% · 1,000 bps
Paid inSOL
LastsAs long as the coin trades

A Stack is not a token. It doesn’t sit in your wallet’s token list; it lives in the coin’s launch account, and this site reads it from there. That keeps it cheap and makes the ownership rules simple: one owner per Stack, changed only by buying it at auction or by its owner sending it.

Unsold Stacks still earn. Fees paid into a Stack’s vault before anyone buys it stay in the vault and go to whoever takes it.

03

Launching on pump.fun

The launch form creates a normal pump.fun coin. It shows up on pump.fun and trades there like any other. The difference is where its creator fees go. Your wallet asks once and signs all three transactions together; the site then sends them in order.

  1. Create the coin

    Pump’s create_v2, plus your optional dev buy in the same transaction, so nobody can buy before you. Your image and metadata are pinned to IPFS first.

  2. Register the Stacks and turn on fee sharing

    The Spot program records the launch and checks on-chain that you are the coin’s creator, so nobody can register someone else’s coin. Then Pump’s create_fee_sharing_config moves the coin’s creator fees into a sharing config.

  3. Point the fees at ten vaults, lock them, open the auction

    Pump’s update_fee_shares sets the shareholders to the ten Stack vaults at 1,000 bps each. Pump allows that list to be set once and then locks it. In the same transaction, open_auction_pump reads Pump’s config and refuses to open unless all ten vaults are there at exactly 10% and the config is locked.

Stopped halfway? If you close your wallet after the coin is created but before step 3 lands, the coin exists but its Stacks aren’t on sale yet. Re-running setup from the same wallet picks up where it stopped (finishPumpSetup in src/chain/pump.ts).

What you need

  • A Solana wallet: Phantom, Solflare or Backpack.
  • A name (32 characters max), a ticker (10 max) and a square image under 5 MB.
  • A narrative: the story of your coin, up to 500 characters. It’s saved in the coin’s metadata, so pump.fun, wallets and the Spot market all show it. It’s the first thing a Stack buyer reads.
  • About 0.03 SOL for rent and network fees, plus the pre-buy fee and your dev buy.

04

The auction

Stacks are sold one at a time in a descending-price (Dutch) auction. The price starts high and drops a fixed amount every 15 seconds until it reaches a floor. The first person to take it pays the current price. Then the clock restarts at the top for the next Stack.

price = max( floor, start − drop × ⌊ seconds since last take ÷ 15 ⌋ )
ModeStartFloorDrop / 15 sStart → floor
Degen1.5 SOL0.5 SOL0.125 SOL2 min
Builder4.0 SOL1.0 SOL0.2 SOL3 min 45 s

You can’t overpay. When you take a Stack you sign a maximum price: the price you saw. If someone takes it first, the price restarts higher, your maximum is too low, and the transaction fails. You pay nothing but the network fee.

Where the money goes. The sale price goes to the coin’s creator, minus a 2% protocol fee to the treasury. The program pays both directly in the same transaction.

Pre-buys. A creator can keep up to five Stacks at launch. They pay only the 2% fee on the floor price for each, because the rest of the price would be paid to themselves.

05

Earning & claiming

Fees reach your Stack in two steps, and anyone can run either one.

Collect

Pump → vaults

Pump holds creator fees until someone calls its distribute_creator_fees. The Collect fees button does that, and pays all ten vaults their 10% at once. After the coin graduates to PumpSwap, it first moves the AMM’s creator fees back to Pump.

Claim

Your vault → you

Claim sends everything in your Stack’s vault above its rent reserve to your wallet. Only the Stack’s current owner can claim it. There’s no deadline: unclaimed SOL waits in the vault.

Because collecting pays every vault, one holder pressing Collect fees helps all ten. If you hold several Stacks of the same coin, collect once, then claim each.

06

Sending & trading

Today a Stack changes hands in two ways: buying it in the auction, or its owner sending it with transfer_stack. Use Send in My Stacks. The Stack moves together with any SOL still unclaimed in its vault, so claim first if you want to keep that.

Because a send is one signed transaction, two people can trade a Stack over the counter: the buyer pays, the seller sends. That needs trust between them. An on-chain order book, where a sale and the payment settle in one transaction, is the next thing on the roadmap.

Valuing a Stack. A Stack earns 10% of creator fees. Pump’s creator fee is a small percentage of every trade, so a Stack’s income tracks the coin’s trading volume. No volume, no fees.

08

What’s enforced on-chain

Buyers shouldn’t have to trust the creator or this website. These checks run inside the Spot program:

  • Only the real creator can register a coin. create_launch reads Pump’s bonding curve and requires the signer to be its creator.
  • The auction can’t open until fees are locked to the vaults. open_auction_pump reads Pump’s sharing config and requires all ten vault addresses at 1,000 bps each, an active config, and a revoked admin.
  • Vaults only pay their owner. claim requires the signer to own that Stack. No one else can withdraw, including the creator and the protocol.
  • No overpaying. take_stack fails if the price is above the maximum you signed.

Check it yourself

On Solscan, open the coin’s fee-sharing config (the sharing-config account of Pump’s fee program for the mint). You should see ten shareholders at 1,000 bps each, and the admin revoked. Each shareholder address is a Stack vault derived from ["vault", mint, i].

09

Risks

Unaudited code

The Spot program has not been audited. Bugs could lose funds.

Volume can go to zero

A Stack only earns while the coin trades. Most coins stop trading.

Pump can change

Pump runs its own programs and admin tools, including for community takeovers. Spot can’t prevent changes Pump makes.

Fees aren’t automatic

Someone has to press Collect fees. Until then, fees wait at Pump.

No order book yet

Selling a Stack after the auction needs a buyer you trust.

Regulation

Selling shares of an income stream may be regulated where you live.

Without VITE_RPC_URL and VITE_TREASURY the site runs as a preview with sample data. Image uploads need PINATA_JWT on the server.

FAQ

Can the creator take the fees back?

No. Once the auction opens, Pump’s fee-sharing list is locked to the ten vaults, and only each Stack’s owner can withdraw from its vault. A creator who pre-bought Stacks earns from those, like any holder.

Does the coin trade differently from other pump.fun coins?

No. It’s a standard pump.fun coin on the same bonding curve, and it graduates to PumpSwap the same way. Only the creator-fee recipient is different.

What happens when the coin graduates?

Creator fees from PumpSwap trades still belong to the coin’s sharing config. Collect fees moves them back to Pump and distributes them to the vaults in one go.

What if nobody buys a Stack?

It stays on sale at the floor price. Its vault keeps collecting fees, and the eventual buyer gets them.

What makes a good narrative?

Say why the coin exists and who it’s for, in a sentence or two. Stack buyers are paying for future trading volume, so tell them why people will keep trading it: a community, a running joke, a product, a cause.

Can I hold more than one Stack?

Yes. Take as many as you like, up to all ten.

Why one wallet prompt for three transactions?

The site asks your wallet to sign all three together, then sends them in order and waits for each to land. They are separate only because together they don’t fit Solana’s size limit for one transaction.
